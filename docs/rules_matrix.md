**Newest Lochac version:** I found nothing newer than v3.4 for armoured combat. The Lochac wiki's news items after July 2024 cover fencing (Oct 2025), the wiki migration (Oct 2025) and youth combat (Jan 2026), not adult armoured rules.

**Hierarchy:** Society 1.2.1(b) says kingdoms "cannot reduce or disregard the requirements and standards delineated in the Society handbooks without a variance". Lochac sits under the affiliates SCA Australia Ltd and SCA NZ Inc, and affiliates may request a deferral (SCA-HB-UPDATES). I found no deferral or variance for the helm rules. The only known variance is the 2011 combat-archery exemption.

The Gauge glossary note says commercial 16 ga is often "0.058 or even 0.055 inch" (1.47 / 1.40 mm), which is "much too thin for helms."

> **External fact, not from the rules:** US Manufacturers' Standard Gauge gives 16 ga = 0.0598 in (1.52 mm) and 14 ga = 0.0747 in (1.90 mm). Buying "16 gauge" therefore fails the 1.6 mm rule, and buying "14 gauge" falls short of a literal 2.0 mm.

## 3. Open questions / conflicts

1. **Lochac v3.4 is older than the 2025/2026 Society helm text.** On these points the Society rule is stricter than Lochac and so, under Society 1.2.1(b), it should apply:
   - "must" 2.0 mm stock for thinning processes;
   - cold-rolled steel;
   - 38.1 mm rivet pitch for small rivets, and blind rivets banned;
   - 12.7 mm padding where the helm touches the neck or body;
   - the dowel may not enter any opening (Lochac only says it may not contact the face).

   SCA-HB-UPDATES lets affiliates defer changes. **I found no Lochac adoption announcement or deferral. Confirm with the Lochac Kingdom Armoured Combat Marshal (armoured@lochac.sca.org).** In the meantime, design to the stricter value.
2. **Interior-mounted face guards.** Lochac 7.5.6 discourages them; Society removed that rule. This is unresolved, so mount the grille on the exterior.
3. **Bar diameter inconsistency.** Lochac 7.5.4 says 4.76 mm but the Lochac glossary says "3/16 inch (4.5mm)". The span condition is "< 2 in" in the rule body but "2 inches or less" in both glossaries. Use 4.76 mm and a span strictly under 50.8 mm.
4. **Perforated plate open area.** The Figure 7.3 captions say "50% Open area" while the rule says "less than 50%". Design to < 50 %.
5. **Is perforated 1.6 mm plate "equivalent to 1.6mm steel"** under 7.5.3? If not, it needs a legal grille underneath. The rules do not say. Ask the Kingdom Armoured Combat Marshal (KACM).
6. **Rear-of-skull / occipital coverage** has no numeric definition anywhere. It is covered only by "bones of the skull". The fit evaluator needs a project-defined headform landmark, for example below the inion. That choice is a project decision, not a rule.
7. **Hardness and high-strength steels.** Neither source mentions hardness. Equivalence is decided by the EM (Society 12.4.2.4(a)). Lochac experimental programmes need EM approval plus approval from the SCA Ltd and SCANZ boards.
8. **Status of the 2011 combat-archery exemption.** It is still hosted on the current Lochac wiki, but I could not independently confirm it is still in force.
9. **Unverified items.** The given sca.org.nz URLs no longer serve the documents; I verified the same content at rules.marshal.lochac.sca.org. lochac.sca.org Earl Marshal pages other than marshal.lochac.sca.org (which is stale) were not checked.

## 4. Implications for a laser-cut plate grille

**Rules (factual):**
- Plate grilles are a recognised face-guard type: Lochac 7.5.3 refers to "a legal **plate or bar grille** face guard".
- **No rule in either source addresses laser-cut plate grilles specifically.** No minimum web width, kerf, heat-affected-zone or edge-finish rule exists.
- The bar rule (7.5.4 / 12.4.3.3) is written for round "bars … in diameter, or equivalent". How it applies to rectangular plate webs is a matter of marshal or EM judgement under "or equivalent". **Needs confirmation with the KACM.**
- The plate is part of "your helm, including the face guard", so it must be **≥ 1.6 mm when completed**. Measure after deburring or grinding.
- Every aperture, including eye slots, must stop a 25.4 mm dowel from entering. That means the largest inscribed circle must be < 25.4 mm (Society 12.4.3.1, 27.3.6.2(b)). Under Lochac 7.5.2 the dowel must also not reach the face.
- The face guard must extend ≥ 25.4 mm below the chin/jaw line (7.5.1).
- All cut edges must be free of sharp edges (7.1 / 12.1.8).
- For Lochac arrow and bolt scenarios, any opening over 5 mm must be screened (7.5.7). A grille cut as perforated plate (t ≥ 1.6 mm, holes ≤ 5 mm, open area < 50 %) is itself a listed screening type (7.5.7(a)). Whether it also removes the need for an underlying grille is open question 5.
- For Lochac only: an exterior attachment is preferred (7.5.6).

**[OPINION] Engineering equivalence estimate.** This is not a rule; it is a first-pass sizing aid only. The assumption is a flat web bent through its thickness by a face-on blow.

To match the elastic bending strength (section modulus Z) of a round bar, the web width must be at least w = 6·Z_bar / t².
- Z for a 4.76 mm round bar is about 10.6 mm³.
- Z for a 3.18 mm round bar is about 3.1 mm³.

Using plastic modulus instead (round bar d³/6, rectangle w·t²/4) gives somewhat larger widths.

| Plate t | Web width w for a 4.76 mm bar | Web width w for a 3.18 mm bar (span < 50.8 mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.6 mm | ≈ 25 mm (plastic ≈ 28) | ≈ 7.4 mm (plastic ≈ 8.3) |
| 2.0 mm | ≈ 16 mm (plastic ≈ 18) | ≈ 4.7 mm (plastic ≈ 5.3) |
| 3.0 mm | ≈ 7.1 mm (plastic ≈ 8.0) | ≈ 2.1 mm (plastic ≈ 2.4) |

- **Bending stiffness** (second moment of area) is far harder to match with thin plate. A 1.6 mm web would need to be about 74 mm wide to equal a 4.76 mm bar's I of about 25 mm⁴.
- Curvature or flanging of a pressed grille, and a 2-D web network, add a lot of stiffness. That should be checked by FEA or a physical test, for example comparing against a reference bar grille under a rattan impact.
- The numbers suggest a **thicker grille plate (≥ 3 mm) or a formed or flanged plate** is the practical way to argue "equivalent" while keeping useful sight lines.
- Take a documented equivalency case (calculation plus test) to the Lochac KACM/EM before production.

**[OPINION] Inner-face clearance.** Society rules allow weapons to intrude up to 12.7 mm into a legal face guard. The grille's inner surface should therefore clear the face by more than 12.7 mm plus liner compression.
